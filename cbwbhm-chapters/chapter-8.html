Chapter 8 — Shuangyun

Cute Beast World: Beast Husband, Memeda!

Lin HuanHuan ran.

Unfortunately, she was nowhere near fast enough to outpace Shuangyun.

She'd barely taken two steps when he caught her by the hair from behind.

"Where do you think you're going?!"

Lin HuanHuan's hair was soft and silky — holding it was like clutching a bundle of seaweed. Shuangyun paused, momentarily thrown.

Every female he'd encountered had always been terribly lazy. They rarely washed, and their hair was perpetually tangled and grimy, like a pile of dry straw.

But this female's hair was clean. Genuinely clean.

And not just her hair — her skin was fair and completely clear of grime.

Shuangyun's grip reflexively loosened a little, though his voice stayed firm. "Since this is your first offence, I'll give you a chance to make it right. Admit what you did and apologize to the witch doctor, and I'll let you go."

Lin HuanHuan's scalp was aching, but she refused to budge.

She hadn't stolen anything. So why should she apologize?!

"You hateful person! Let me go!"

Shuangyun felt his temper flare again at her continued defiance. "Say that again — I dare you. See if I don't hit you!"

"You absolute brute!" Lin HuanHuan glared at him. "If you're going to hit me, then just beat me to death and get it over with!"

Shuangyun raised his fist — and found he couldn't bring it down on her face.

But letting her go of his own accord was equally out of the question. He wasn't about to give in like that.

His mind went around in circles, and still he couldn't find it in himself to actually do anything. In the end, he could only force the words out stiffly.

"Just admit it. Then I'll let you go."

"I have nothing to admit!" Lin HuanHuan stared back at him, unyielding. "You could kill me and I still wouldn't admit it!"

…

While the two were locked in their standoff, Lang Zhu finally returned.

He'd gone to Shuangyun's cave first, hoping to introduce him to the little female — but Shuangyun hadn't been home. Lang Zhu had turned back empty-handed.

What he walked in on was the last thing he expected.

Shuangyun had one hand twisted in Lin HuanHuan's hair and his other fist raised in the air, his face dark with fury — looking for all the world as though he was about to strike her.

Lang Zhu nearly dropped to his knees in fright.

He rushed forward and shoved them apart.

"What in the world is wrong with you?!" he snapped at Shuangyun, his face etched with distress. "How could you be so cruel to a girl like this? Is your heart made of stone?!"

"She was stealing from your house," Shuangyun said, frowning. "I was teaching her a lesson on your behalf."

"Stealing?! There's nothing worth stealing in this old wreck of a house!"

Lang Zhu was so furious his beard practically bristled. "This girl is injured. I asked her to stay here and rest. And you — you come in here and start beating people at random! Are you trying to be the death of me?!"

Shuangyun went still.

"She… she's not a thief? I thought she was here to steal something, I didn't realize—"

"Out! Get out of my sight!" Lang Zhu brandished his cane at him and drove him toward the door. "Don't come back until I've cooled down. Looking at you right now makes my blood boil!"

Shuangyun stood at the door and watched it slam in his face. He slowly raised one hand and touched his nose.

A shadow passed over his expression.

He lingered outside for a while. When it became clear that Lang Zhu had no intention of opening the door again, he finally turned and walked away.

…

"You might not know much about Shuangyun," Lang Zhu said to Lin HuanHuan. "That boy has a bad temper — a bit more hotheaded than your average beastman — but he's not a bad person at heart. He's extraordinarily strong, and already the youngest chief in the history of the wolf clan."

Lin HuanHuan was tying her hair back with a sturdy-looking blade of grass.

She shook her head lightly, and the little ponytail swayed.

"It's fine. I'm not angry anymore. I won't be seeing him again anyway."

Lang Zhu gave a small cough. "Shuangyun was in the wrong this time. I'll have him come and apologize. And if that's not enough, you're welcome to hit him — don't hold back. A good beating never hurt anyone!"

Lin HuanHuan was speechless.

She really wasn't angry anymore.

She didn't want to hit anyone. She just wanted to wait for Bai Di to come back so they could leave together.

But why wasn't Bai Di back yet?

Lin HuanHuan stared at the door.

Lang Zhu kept putting in a good word for Shuangyun, doing his best to nudge the two young people together. But Lin HuanHuan remained completely unmoved — she truly didn't seem to have any interest in Shuangyun whatsoever.

Lang Zhu cursed the boy silently. That wretched little brat, raising his hand against a female. Now he'd gone and scared off his best chance at a mate. What was going to become of him? Was he just going to stay alone until he shrivelled up and died?!

…

Bai Di finally came back.

Lin HuanHuan leapt off the bed, flung herself into his arms, and beamed up at him. "You're finally back!"

Bai Di set her gently back onto the bed and held out her cloth bag. "I brought your things."

"What took you so long?" she asked, taking the bag.

"I stopped to wash the hide I tanned yesterday." He unfolded it and laid it in front of her. "I know you like collecting animal skins, so I brought it back for you."

Lin HuanHuan shifted a little uncomfortably. "I don't really have a hobby of collecting animal skins. You don't need to gather more for me in the future."

Bai Di smiled and didn't ask why she'd wanted animal skins yesterday but not today.

"If you don't want it anymore, we'll leave it."

He tossed the hide aside. Then, like a magician producing a trick, he brought a small bouquet of flowers out from behind his back.

Tiny red blooms, still jewelled with morning dew, carrying a faint, sweet fragrance.

"Are these for me?" Lin HuanHuan's face lit up. "They're beautiful!"

Bai Di placed them in her hands. "As long as you like them."

"Thank you!"

"I've told you before," Bai Di said with gentle exasperation, "we're family. No need to thank me."

Lin HuanHuan stuck her tongue out at him, grinning cheekily.

…

Shuangyun stood at the door and watched the scene through the crack.

The little female was smiling, cradling the flowers in her hands.

He'd thought she was a wild cat — beautiful, yes, but always with her teeth bared and claws out.

But now that he knew he'd been wrong about her, he could see she only showed her claws to people who had wronged her. For the people she cared for, she was gentle and soft — sweet enough to make your chest ache.

Something sour twisted in Shuangyun's heart.

If he'd known she wasn't a thief, he never would have handled her so roughly.

She must hate him now.

Shuangyun looked down at the small animal-skin pouch in his hand. It was full of plump red berries, every one of them washed carefully clean. He'd picked them himself, meaning to give them to her as an apology.

But now, pierced through the heart by her smile, he didn't dare show his face in front of her again.

Shuangyun quietly set the pouch down on the doorstep, gave the door a light knock, and slipped away.

When Bai Di opened the door, there was no one there — only the small animal-skin pouch sitting on the ground.

He picked it up and looked inside at the red berries, puzzled.

"Who sent this?"
