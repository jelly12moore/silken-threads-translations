Chapter 9 — Bachelor For Life

Cute Beast World: Beast Husband, Memeda!

"Who was it?" Lin HuanHuan asked curiously.

"I don't know — when I opened the door, the person was already gone." Bai Di set the pouch down in front of her. "This was left on the doorstep. It must have been from them."

Lin HuanHuan looked at the pouch, brimming over with red berries, and couldn't hide her delight. "So many!"

"Would you like some?" Bai Di asked.

"No," Lin HuanHuan shook her head. "Someone left these at the witch doctor's door. They must be his."

"You could still have some. I can always pick more berries and replace them."

"There's no need — the ones the witch doctor gave me aren't even finished yet. We should return these. It's not right to take things that belong to someone else."

"Alright," Bai Di said. He set the pouch aside, then reached out and touched Lin HuanHuan's backside with a slight frown. "Is it still bleeding?"

Lin HuanHuan went red. "It usually takes five days. This is only day two — it's still early."

"With so much blood, are you really sure you won't die?" Bai Di's face was creased with worry.

"I won't. I've been like this every month for years without any problems."

Looking at Lin HuanHuan's complete confidence, Bai Di had no choice but to swallow down his anxiety for the time being.

Before long, Lang Zhu returned.

He asked Lin HuanHuan about her condition as he always did, and once he confirmed there was nothing out of the ordinary, the tension in the room eased.

Bai Di handed the berry pouch to Lang Zhu. "This was left at your door a little while ago. It should belong to you."

Lang Zhu took a couple of the berries and turned them over in his hand. He brought them to his nose and sniffed — and his wrinkled face broke into a sly, knowing smile.

He pressed the pouch back into Lin HuanHuan's hands. "These are for you. Keep them."

"Aren't they yours?" Lin HuanHuan asked, surprised.

"Trust me. They're definitely for you."

Before Lin HuanHuan could ask anything else, Lang Zhu turned and hobbled away with his cane.

At the back of the dwelling, he found Shuangyun perched on a large boulder.

The tall, handsome young man sat with one knee drawn up, his honey-toned skin glowing warmly in the light of the setting sun. The breeze stirred his short silver hair, softening the usual sharpness of his features.

Though Lang Zhu's own hair and beard had long since gone white, he was still remarkably spry. He hopped up onto the rock with ease, then reached over and rapped his knuckles against Shuangyun's forehead.

"You rotten boy," he said, though his tone carried a smile. "Lifting a hand against a female. You really ought to be ashamed of yourself."

Shuangyun sat perfectly still. He slid Lang Zhu a sideways glance and let out a short sound. "If someone does something wrong, I'll deal with them — female or not."

"But she didn't do anything wrong. You misunderstood her completely."

Shuangyun pressed his lips together and said nothing.

"Go and apologize. Coax her a little."

"I don't grovel to females," Shuangyun said, turning away.

Lang Zhu watched him with amusement. "You obviously like the girl, and yet you're sitting here pretending to be annoyed. Who taught you to be so pig-headed?"

"Like her?" Shuangyun's voice went sharp. "Not possible. I hate all females!"

"Is that so," Lang Zhu said pleasantly. "Then why did you secretly send her a bag of red berries?"

"What red berries?" Shuangyun answered immediately. "I have no idea what you're talking about."

"Still playing dumb? Your scent is all over them. I could smell it the moment I touched the bag."

The tips of Shuangyun's ears went faintly pink, though his mouth remained defiant. "I picked those up while I was out hunting. They were meant for you, not for her."

"In that case, I'll go back inside and ask her to return them to me."

He actually moved to get down from the rock. Shuangyun grabbed his arm.

"Don't!"

Lang Zhu looked at him with a smile. "And why not?"

Shuangyun's hands balled into fists. He looked like he had a great deal to say. In the end, only a few words made it out.

"I really did just pick them up…"

"Red berries grow inside thorn bushes, and those thorns are venomous. Even animals don't go near them. What hunter just casually picks berries from a thorn bush in passing? Even the youngest cubs in this tribe wouldn't swallow that story."

Shuangyun looked down at the back of his hand, punctured by thorns, swollen and red, still itching fiercely.

He said nothing. His silver-white hair fell forward, obscuring his eyes. His jaw was tight.

Lang Zhu produced a handful of leaves and held them out. "Chew these and press them into the wounds. It'll bring down the swelling and stop the itch."

Shuangyun took the leaves without a word.

…

Lin HuanHuan stayed with the wolf tribe for five days, and her aunt finally took her reluctant leave.

"Your wound is healed!" Bai Di said, visibly relieved. "You're not going to die!"

"I was never wounded," Lin HuanHuan said for what felt like the hundredth time. "And I was never going to die."

Bai Di scooped her up and ruffled her hair.

With the aunt gone, there was no longer any reason to stay in the wolf tribe.

Bai Di took her to say their farewells to Lang Zhu.

"No, no, no — you can't leave yet!" Lang Zhu said at once.

It had taken considerable effort to get that boy Shuangyun even remotely interested in a female. If she left now, what was going to become of him? Were the gods truly determined to see him die a bachelor?!

He had to find a way to keep Lin HuanHuan from leaving.

"Why can't she leave?" Bai Di asked.

Lang Zhu thought fast, and something struck him. "Winter is coming!"

"Winter is still two months away," Bai Di said, frowning. "We have plenty of time."

"In past years, yes — two months would be plenty. But this year is different. The season has shifted. Winter will arrive two months early. I've deduced this from the movement of the stars, and I am absolutely certain. There is no room for error."

As a witch doctor, Lang Zhu possessed skills beyond medicine — among them, the ability to read the weather. His predictions were not always precise under ordinary circumstances, but when it came to major events — great snowstorms, floods, disasters — they were unerringly accurate.

It was precisely this gift that had allowed the wolf tribe to avoid countless catastrophes over the years, growing into the large and thriving settlement it was today.

Lang Zhu's words gave Bai Di genuine pause.

At that same moment, a voice chimed in Lin HuanHuan's mind.

[Serial Mission Triggered: The Long Winter!]

[A bitter cold is on its way. Host, please prepare accordingly. The system will release a series of tasks throughout the winter season. Complete them to receive generous rewards!]

The moment she heard there were rewards, Lin HuanHuan's eyes lit up.

The fire starter and atlas she'd received for completing the beginner tasks had both proven incredibly useful. If this was a full serial mission, the rewards were bound to be something spectacular.
